### **4. Proof Logic**

The proof follows a classic *maximal counterexample* strategy:

1. **Assume for contradiction** that not all ideals are principal → define `nonPrincipals`.
2. **Apply Zorn’s Lemma** to get a *maximal* non-principal ideal `I`.
3. **Show `I` is prime**:
   - Suppose `x * y ∈ I`, `y ∉ I`.
   - Consider `I ⊔ span{y}` — strictly larger than `I`, hence principal by maximality.
   - Consider `I : span{y}` (colon ideal) — also strictly larger unless equal to `I`.
   - Show both being principal implies `I` is principal (via generator `a * b`), contradiction.
4. **Conclude** that all ideals are principal, since any non-principal ideal would yield a prime non-principal ideal, contradicting the hypothesis.

This mirrors the standard algebraic proof that “if all prime ideals are principal, then the ring is a PIR”.
