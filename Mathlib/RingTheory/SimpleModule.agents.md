### Technical Metadata Brief: Simple and Semisimple Modules in Lean 4 (Mathlib)

---

#### **1. Key Definitions & Theorems**

| Name | Type / Signature | Purpose |
|------|------------------|---------|
| `IsSimpleModule` | `abbrev IsSimpleModule := IsSimpleOrder (Submodule R M)` | Indicates that the only submodules of `M` are `⊥` and `⊤`. |
| `IsSemisimpleModule` | `abbrev IsSemisimpleModule := ComplementedLattice (Submodule R M)` | Every submodule has a complement; equivalently, `M` is a direct sum of simple submodules. |
| `IsSemisimpleRing` | `abbrev IsSemisimpleRing := IsSemisimpleModule R R` | A ring is semisimple if it is semisimple as a module over itself. |
| `bijective_or_eq_zero` | `∀ f : M →ₗ[R] N, Function.Bijective f ∨ f = 0` | **Schur’s Lemma**: A linear map between simple modules is either bijective or zero. |
| `isSimpleModule_iff_quot_maximal` | `IsSimpleModule R M ↔ ∃ I : Ideal R, I.IsMaximal ∧ Nonempty (M ≃ₗ[R] R ⧸ I)` | Characterizes simple modules as quotients by maximal left ideals. |
| `sSup_simples_eq_top_iff_isSemisimpleModule` | `sSup { m | IsSimpleModule R m } = ⊤ ↔ IsSemisimpleModule R M` | A module is semisimple iff it is generated by its simple submodules. |
| `annihilator_isRadical` | `[CommRing R] → IsSemisimpleModule R M → (annihilator R M).IsRadical` | Annihilator of a semisimple module over a commutative ring is a radical ideal. |
| `IsSemisimpleModule.submodule`, `quotient` | Instances showing closure under submodules and quotients | Any submodule or quotient of a semisimple module is semisimple. |
| `isSemisimpleModule_of_isSemisimpleModule_submodule` | `∀ i ∈ s, IsSemisimpleModule R (p i) ∧ ⨆ p i = ⊤ → IsSemisimpleModule R M` | A module generated by semisimple submodules is semisimple. |
| `IsSemisimpleRing.isSemisimpleModule` | `[IsSemisimpleRing R] → IsSemisimpleModule R M` | Every module over a semisimple ring is semisimple. |
| `instIsSemisimpleRingForAllRing` | `[∀ i, IsSemisimpleRing (R i)] → IsSemisimpleRing (∀ i, R i)` | Finite product of semisimple rings is semisimple. |
| `RingHom.isSemisimpleRing_of_surjective` | `[IsSemisimpleRing R] → Function.Surjective f → IsSemisimpleRing S` | Quotients of semisimple rings are semisimple. |
| `divisionRing_end` | `[IsSimpleModule R M] → DivisionRing (Module.End R M)` | Endomorphism ring of a simple module is a division ring (Schur’s Lemma corollary). |

#### **2. Naming Conventions**

- **Prefixes**:
  - `isSimpleModule_`, `isSemisimpleModule_`, `IsSimpleModule.`, `IsSemisimpleModule.` — for properties and lemmas about simplicity/semisimplicity.
  - `annihilator_` — for results involving module annihilators.
  - `quot_`, `comap_`, `map_` — for constructions involving quotient modules and pullbacks/pushforwards of submodules.

- **Suffixes**:
  - `_iff` — for biconditional characterizations (e.g., `isSimpleModule_iff_quot_maximal`).
  - `_eq_top`, `_eq_bot` — for conditions involving top/bottom submodules.
  - `_surjective`, `_injective`, `_bijective` — for properties of linear maps.
  - `_isMaximal`, `_isAtom`, `_isCoatom` — for order-theoretic properties of submodules.

- **Other patterns**:
  - `congr` — for transport of structure along isomorphisms.
  - `of_` — for implications in one direction (e.g., `of_sSup_simples_eq_top`).
  - `sup`, `inf`, `biSup`, `sSup` — for lattice-theoretic constructions.

#### **4. Proof Logic & Strategy**

- **Induction / Lattice Theory**: Many proofs rely on lattice-theoretic characterizations:
  - `IsSimpleModule R m ↔ IsAtom m`
  - `IsSemisimpleModule R M ↔ ComplementedLattice (Submodule R M)`
  - Use of `sSup_atoms_eq_top`, `complementedLattice_of_sSup_atoms_eq_top`, etc.

- **Case Analysis on Submodule Lattice**:
  - `eq_bot_or_eq_top` is heavily used for simple modules.
  - `covBy_iff_coatom_Iic`, `isSimpleModule_iff_isCoatom` for quotient simplicity.

- **Isomorphism Transport**:
  - `Submodule.orderIsoMapComap`, `LinearMap.quotKerEquivOfSurjective`, `quotientEquivOfIsCompl` used to transfer properties across isomorphisms.

- **Annihilator Calculations**:
  - `Submodule.annihilator_iSup`, `I.annihilator_quotient`, `e.annihilator_eq` for ideal-theoretic properties.

- **Module Generation**:
  - Use of `Finsupp`, `range`, `iSup`, `biSup`, and `span` to express generation by simple submodules.

- **Ring-Theoretic Constructions**:
  - `Module.compHom`, `Pi.evalRingHom`, `single`, `range_eq_map`, `Submodule.pi_top` for product/quotient ring arguments.

Module theory over arbitrary rings (not necessarily commutative), with emphasis on:
- Simplicity and semisimplicity,
- Schur’s Lemma and its consequences,
