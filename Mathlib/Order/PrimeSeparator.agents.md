#### 4. **Proof Logic / Strategy**

- **High-level structure**:
  1. **Setup**: Define the poset `S` of ideals extending `I` and disjoint from `F`.
  2. **Non-emptiness**: Show `I ∈ S`.
  3. **Chain upper bounds**: Use `sUnion` and properties of ideals (closed under directed sups) to show every nonempty chain in `S` has an upper bound in `S`.
  4. **Apply Zorn’s Lemma**: Obtain a maximal element `J ∈ S`.
  5. **Prove primality**:
     - Assume `a₁, a₂ ∉ J`, aim to show `a₁ ⊔ a₂ ∉ J`.
     - Consider extensions `J₁ = J ⊔ ⟨a₁⟩`, `J₂ = J ⊔ ⟨a₂⟩`.
     - Maximality implies `Jᵢ ∉ S`, so each intersects `F`.
     - Pick `cᵢ ∈ F ∩ Jᵢ`, lift to `bᵢ ∈ J` with `cᵢ ≤ bᵢ ⊔ aᵢ`.
     - Use distributivity to get `c₁ ⊓ c₂ ≤ b ⊔ (a₁ ⊓ a₂)` where `b = b₁ ⊔ b₂ ∈ J`.
     - Since `c₁ ⊓ c₂ ∈ F`, and `F` is an upper set, `b ⊔ (a₁ ⊓ a₂) ∈ F`.
     - If `a₁ ⊓ a₂ ∈ J`, then `b ⊔ (a₁ ⊓ a₂) ∈ J`, contradicting `F ∩ J = ∅`.
     - Hence `a₁ ⊓ a₂ ∉ J`, and by contrapositive, `a₁ ⊔ a₂ ∉ J`.

- **Key logical tools**:
  - Zorn’s Lemma (`zorn_subset_nonempty`)
  - Contrapositive reasoning (`contrapose!`)
  - Distributive lattice identities (`sup_inf_left`)
  - Filter/ideal closure properties (`sup_mem`, `inf_mem`, `PFilter.mem_of_le`)

### Summary

This formalization proves a foundational separation result in lattice theory: disjoint filters and ideals can be separated by a prime ideal. The proof is constructive in the sense of classical logic (uses Zorn’s Lemma), and heavily leverages distributivity and filter/ideal closure properties. The naming and structure follow Lean/Mathlib conventions, with a clear separation of setup, maximality argument, and primality verification.
